What blocks what

Every connected component in the live blocking graph, laid out by execution layer. Columns are what must happen in order; rows within a column are what can run at once.

How it has proceeded

Retries, reclaims and poisonings. Every individual failure here read as routine when it happened; the runaway is only visible as a distribution over the whole database.

What arrives, and how long it stays

Fourteen days of arrivals, and how long the work now open has been open. Completions are counted over closed beads, and no closed bead is fetched.

How this page works

One read endpoint returns the live beads. Everything else on this page — the blocks, the radius, the connected components, the layers, the buckets and every headline count — is computed here, in the browser, from that array.

01
The server returns beads, and nothing that has been chewed

No coordinates, no components, no layers, no histograms, no counts. A payload carrying those needs a rendering stack on the server and gives the two ends a second place to disagree; the measurement that decided it is below. The one thing the server owns is the query.

02
Nothing runs when nobody is looking

There is no regenerator, no snapshot on disk and no daemon. The only thing that runs is the request. A short cache on the read path bounds the cost by time rather than by viewer, so ten open tabs and a page that re-fetches cost one query — a cache, not a background job — and the endpoint records every query's wall time and refuses over budget rather than serving late.

03
The client paints, and it was measured before it was decided

The whole pass — treemap, packing, edge routing, components, layering and every bucket — is single-digit milliseconds at a few hundred beads and tens of milliseconds at twenty thousand. Server-side layout would buy nothing at that price and cost a rendering stack. Try it: the corpus control on the Map replays the same views against a synthetic graph at 1k, 5k and 20k.

04
Bead size is solved, not configured

The ink fraction is the constant, not the radius. Blocks are sized to hold their beads at the solved radius and flowed in justified rows, which holds the ink roughly fixed from a few hundred beads to twenty thousand. Past the measured legibility floor the stage stops shrinking beads and starts aggregating — to epics, then to repositories — and clicking a block drills in.

05
Every interaction is a URL, and so is every refresh

The page writes its state to the location hash and reads it back, so a link restores the same view. “What moved” needs no stored history either: each bead carries its own last-touched time, so the delta is a window the reader chooses rather than a diff the server keeps. No push, no stream — a page left open re-fetches on its own schedule.

06
One well-known address, and it is not a public one

A user service on a fixed port, reachable on the local network and mappable through a tunnel so it works away from the house. The beads are not public. They carry internal working notes, agent memories and the operator's own judgement, so a tunnel must sit behind access control — an identity-gated tunnel, never a bare hostname. That constraint is not this page's alone; it is why the database has never shipped with the harness.

Its relationship to the attention surface

This page does not get an attention list. The cockpit is where the operator answers things and where the numbers that surprised us are instrumented; this page's rule is show the shape. Two surfaces answering “is anything wrong” differently is how one of them becomes wallpaper, so anything needing a human is filed as a bead and reaches them there. The needs operator colour here is a filter for orientation, not a queue to work.

What scales, and where it breaks

  • The query is bounded by work in flight, not by history. The call that returns the live beads returns no closed ones, and the closed corpus grows without limit and is never fetched. That bound is what makes a per-request API affordable at all, so it is asserted by a test rather than assumed.
  • The map degrades by aggregating, not by shrinking. Hold the ink fraction constant until the radius hits the legibility floor, then stop: a block becomes a density cell with a count on it.
  • All-edges mode does not scale and is not meant to. It is there to be looked at once, to understand why the dependency cone is the default. Cone stays readable at any density because it traces one bead's reach.
  • Chains and Churn scale by ranking and truncation. Chains draws the largest components and pages; Churn draws the worst rows and counts the rest, because past about thirty rows it stops being a shape and becomes a table.
  • Two panels are missing rather than approximated. Completions per day and created-to-closed cycle time are computed over closed beads, and no closed bead reaches this page. Arrivals and time-in-flight are exact and are what Flow shows; a completions number computed over the open population would read as the number it is named after while measuring something else. The durable source is an append-only event log of landings, closes, reclaims and gate verdicts.
  • Churn's time axis needs that same log. Attempt and reclaim counts are labels on the bead, so the distribution is exact and the history is not. Until the log exists that panel is a sketch and is marked as one.
